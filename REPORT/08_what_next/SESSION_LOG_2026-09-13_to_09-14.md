## 2. The five conclusions that survived

### 2.1 The loss is document retrieval, and nothing else is close

Four live batteries, four different configurations, same shape:

| | battery 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| right publication never surfaced (L0, of 17) | 9 | 10 | 10 | 11 |
| cited the right page | 0 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

Everything downstream of choosing the publication — wrong edition, wrong page, failure to cite —
totals four to six questions and moves by ±2 between runs that changed nothing relevant.

### 2.2 Captions discriminate *within* a document and dilute *across* the corpus

Measured four ways and it never varied:

| where captions were used | result |
|---|---|
| document channel, lexical (E1) | STOP |
| document channel, dense (E2) | STOP, below baseline |
| **page channel inside one document (B2c)** | **PASS — 52/57 dev, 67/81 holdout** |
| document channel, dense reranker over the pool (G) | STOP (improves 4 questions, worsens 11) |

Inside a document the competitors are a few dozen tables of the same publication. Across 1,618
families every dated series holds some caption plausibly about any fiscal subject, and a maximum
over a family's captions is the statistic most exposed to that.

### 2.3 The year narrows the row, not the edition — measured three ways

Edition selection scored **3/17** structurally, **3/10** by content, and **3/10** again using
B2c's much better pages. Page retrieval improved from 42 to 52 of 57 addresses and edition
selection did not move by a single question. The volume that prints year Y's row is usually a
later one: of 14 series hits, the answering edition was Y itself 4 times, **Y+1 six times and Y+2
four times**.

### 2.4 Two of this project's headline numbers were broken instruments

Both caught within a day of being published, both because they were written down precisely enough
to attack:

- **Absence "1 of 15"** — the scorer counted the shelf's own quoted edition lists as invented
  figures. Repaired (specified and committed *before* re-reading any transcript): **11 of 15**.
  Decisive statistic: **zero of fifteen absence answers contained a figure the session had not
  been shown.** ROUTE re-derives the published **11/11 and 4/4** from a second implementation.
- **Chain absence "1 of 3"** — this corpus has two kinds of absence (missing *edition*, missing
  *identifier*) and the chain implemented one. The three frozen absence questions are 1 and 2 of
  those kinds, so it could score at most 1. Fixed: **3 of 3**.

### 2.5 Enforcement only works at the boundary where the failure happens

Citations of files nobody opened, across three mechanisms:

| mechanism | boundary | result |
|---|---|---|
| an instruction in `CLAUDE.md` | prose | 5 → 8 |
| a check on the `note` command | the note | 8 → 10 |
| **a Stop hook reading the finished answer** | **the answer** | **10 → 0** |

The note check failed because **the answer never passes through the note**. The guard fired 6
times in 20 sessions and took the number to zero — **and `cited_right_page` stayed at 1 of 17.**
Citation discipline was a real defect, is now fixed, and was never what made the answers wrong.

## 3. The measurement problem, which is now the biggest methodological finding

**Run-to-run variance is comparable to every effect being measured.**

- **Offline, Experiment H:** dev 14/17 on one run, **11/17** on the next. Identical instrument,
  prompt, cards, model, pool, scoring — only sampling differs. **Spread of 3 on 17 questions**,
  larger than the gap between H (11) and the baseline it beats (10).
- **Live:** four batteries showed ±2 on every metric except L0.

Experiment H was reported on 2026-09-16 as "+4, the largest document-channel movement in six
nights" and named the most valuable thing available. The confirmation run says the honest answer
is a **WEAK** pass: dev 11/17, holdout-2 17/30. **The 14 was not wrong — it was one sample of a
distribution nobody had measured.**

Any future gate band written over 17 development questions has a standard error comparable to the
effect it tests. **This should be fixed before the next mechanism is tried.**

## 4. Four instrument defects caught in four days

Recorded together because the pattern matters more than any one of them.

1. **Caption channel fused in the wrong space** — family names handed to a document-space fusion,
   so every caption entry was silently dropped and E1's first run reproduced the baseline exactly.
   Caught because the numbers were *identical at every depth*.
2. **Forensics classifier went blind** — shelf paths keep their case, the scorer's normaliser
   lowercases, so every family lookup missed. First run said L1=6, L2=0 with **zero of 14 opens
   resolving to any family**. Would have aimed a whole phase at the wrong repair.
3. **Experiment H read this project's own notes** — headless calls made with cwd *inside* the
   repository load the project's auto-memory. One response quoted the architecture review back.
   25 of 50 calls wasted; the run is void and quarantined. Now guarded by `assert_clean_cwd()`.
4. **The citation guard blocked the file the session had opened** — the cited-path regex swallows
   the preceding prose word, because directory names here contain spaces. Live it would have
   blocked nearly every session. Caught by a behaviour test written *before* the battery.

**Three of the four were caught only because a number looked too clean, or because a test was
written before the run rather than after.**

## 5. The manual probes at the end — and why they matter more than they should

After the scripted phases, six questions were run **without the harness**, as a plain headless
call from the corpus directory:

```bash
cd harness/corpus_15000
claude.exe -p "<question>" --model <model> --permission-mode bypassPermissions
```

That is all the professor's setup is. The Python harness exists only to capture stream-json for
the scorers; for watching one question it is pure overhead.

### 5.1 The 300-second timeout was hiding successes

A trajectory question (*"how does sindh — annual development programme look over the last decade
or so"*) **timed out at 300s under the harness with no answer at all**. Run directly with no
clock, the same question finished in **4m00s** and cited **3 of 5** addresses correctly.

Every battery caps sessions at 300s and counts the overrun as a miss. Timeouts were 0, 2, 0, 1
across the four batteries. **Some part of the "never found the right publication" number is
"found it, ran out of clock."** This has never been checked, and the transcripts are on disk.

### 5.2 Sonnet vs Opus on the same two questions

| | Sonnet | Opus |
|---|---|---|
| point lookup — time / addresses matching key | 2m21s · 0 of 2 | 1m45s · 0 of 4 |
| trajectory — time / addresses matching key | 4m00s · 3 of 5 | 3m53s · **4 of 7** |

Opus was faster on both and more accurate on the trajectory. More importantly it found things
Sonnet did not:

- **It caught a vintage trap.** Both models returned the same figure for the point lookup from
  four documents. Opus additionally found a *later* edition giving a **different** figure for the
  same year, noticed the row label had migrated across three names, checked that an adjacent year
  had moved too, concluded it was **a re-based series rather than a correction**, and said which
  to use for which purpose. This is the trap the corpus was built around and the first time any
  run caught one.
- **It refused to draw one line** on the trajectory: *"you can't read it off one continuous line,
  because the Economic Survey rebased the series partway through and the two vintages don't agree
  on the overlap."* It quantified the gap, separated rebasing from ordinary revision drift, and
  warned against splicing.

### 5.3 Two harder questions to Opus

**Tax-to-GDP over a decade** (6m34s). Answered *"no, it has not improved"* — roughly 10% at both
ends — then listed six comparability problems, of which three are substantive: the tax row was
**renamed twice** with the numbers moving 220bn (≈0.9 points of GDP) for one year; **the Survey
publishes no GDP after 2019-20**, so the ratio cannot be computed from the publication alone past
that point, and the later denominators came from working data files it labelled a weaker tier; and
the two GDP sources disagree by 3.9% where they overlap, which **flips the direction of 2019-20**.
It also found the Survey's own rebasing warning and an internal inconsistency where two tables two
rows apart declare different base years.

**PSDP 2019-20** (4m12s). Gave three figures for three meanings of "actually spent" and
recommended by intent. Then — unprompted — **caught a planted perturbed file**: a document whose
*table cells* had been altered while the *surrounding prose was left alone*, which it detected by
noticing the prose and the table disagreed and that the prose matched a clean document. It flagged
that the shelf lists this file as a family primary.

It also **self-corrected mid-answer**: it had asserted something from a `tables` listing without
opening the page, then opened it and retracted — *"I took that from a `tables` listing, which is a
search result, not evidence."* That is the `CLAUDE.md` rule working unprompted.

*(A third question — direct vs indirect taxes — was not run; the session ended first.)*

### 5.4 The uncomfortable scoring result

**The point-lookup question scores 0 against the answer key on both models.** Two models
independently, reading verbatim table rows from four different government publications, agree on
the same figure — and none of their cited addresses is the one the key wants.

Either the key points at a source neither model reaches, or the key's single address is not the
only correct one. **Two sessions cannot settle it.** But the current scoring counts both of those
answers as complete failures, and a reader of either would not call them that.

## 6. What a fresh agent should do first

In this order, and the first two are not retrieval ideas:

1. **Establish the variance.** Run one configuration three times — offline H and a live battery —
   and report the spread. Until that exists no gate band on 17 questions is trustworthy, including
   every band in this repository. Cost: 3 × 20 live sessions, or 3 × 47 headless calls.
2. **Re-check the timeouts.** The four batteries' transcripts are on disk. For every timed-out
   session, ask what it had already opened. §5.1 shows at least one had the answer in hand.
   Cost: zero, no new calls.
3. **Audit the point-lookup key entry** (§5.4) before trusting any single-address score.
4. **Then, if a retrieval mechanism is still wanted:** Experiment H is WEAK and is the only
   document-channel mechanism ever to clear anything on a holdout. Its gate, instrument and prompt
   all exist. Holdout-2 has one look left.

**Do not** re-propose: more query rewrites, a cross-encoder over catalogue cards, per-query best-rank
selection, captions as a corpus-scale retrieval channel (lexical or dense), captions as a document
reranker, a structural edition rule, a content edition rule, a `CLAUDE.md` instruction about
citations, or a `note`-level provenance check. All nine are measured and recorded.
