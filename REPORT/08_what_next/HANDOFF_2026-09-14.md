# Handoff — corrected continuation of experiments A and B, 2026-09-14

Both experiments the 2026-09-13 architecture note proposed were run, after the harness that
produced the numbers they were aimed at was repaired. Counts only below; no paths, no labels,
nothing from the answer key.

**1. What was fixed in the measurement system.**
Four defects. The page row was fed the whole question instead of a short row phrase (D1); two
of the seventeen questions were scored automatic misses because the shelf had collapsed their
evidence file into a byte-identical twin's duplicate list (D2); the "does it correctly say it
*does* hold this edition" control queried the shelf with the shelf's own words and counted a
no-family-match as a success (D3); and per-question ranks were computed and thrown away, so
nothing past the top ten was known (D4). All four are fixed in a separate code path, so every
historical number still reproduces from the original one — and all seven of them did, exactly:
6/17, 8/17, 25/57, 1/4, 11/11, 4/4, 14/17. The replacement for the circular control (F5) asks
the question's own words with a year the publication actually holds swapped in.

**2. The true current baselines.**
CORRECTED, right document in the top ten: **6 of 17** on the question as asked, **9 of 17**
with five paraphrases, with **nothing excluded** (was 8 of 17 with two questions dropped).
Right page in the top five, given the right document: **24 of 57 addresses, 42.1% counting
addresses and 27.9% counting questions**. Trajectory walk: **1 of 4**. Symmetric
present-edition control: **8 of 11**, with 3 unanswerable because the publication holds no
dated edition to swap in.

**3. A0 — candidate generation or ranking?**
**Ranking.** The right document is in the top-100 candidate pool on **16 of 17** questions
(10 at depth 10, 10 at 20, 11 at 50, 16 at 100, 16 at 200), and on 28 of 30 holdout questions.
Depth 200 adds nothing over depth 100. So the pool almost always holds the answer and
something already inside it is failing to surface — which is what authorised the reranker.

**4. Did the reranker run, and what happened.**
Yes. The first model passed its preflight (five everyday question/passage pairs ordered
correctly, 16 pairs a second), so no second model was tried. The cross-encoder on its own put
the right document in the top ten on **4 of 17** — worse than the 7 of 17 pool it was handed —
and 12 of 30 on the holdout. Fused back with the original ranking it reached **9 of 17** and
16 of 30. The pre-registered bar was 12 of 17, with 9 or fewer a stop. **It stopped.** Its 9
is exactly what the pre-registered rule about dropping one paraphrase had cost, so the
reranker bought nothing; the bar was not moved even though the holdout improved.

**5. The clean page baseline.**
**24 of 57 addresses in the top five, 42.1% by address and 27.9% by question.** Fixing the
whole-question defect cost one address, so D1 was a real inconsistency in the code that was
not costing anything on this measure. This, not the old 43.9%, is what a page change must beat.

**6. The caption result, and the series rerun.**
Recorded **WEAK**, and the reason matters more than the band. Captions take the page result
from 24 of 57 to **42 of 57 (73.7%)**, which clears the 60% address bar, but the per-question
figure only reaches 39.7%, which does not — so the pre-registered pass condition, which needed
both, is not met and was not relaxed. The split is because **every single address gained is on
a trajectory question** (22 of 42 to 40 of 42) and not one of the other fifteen moved; on nine
of thirteen questions no caption contains all the query's words at all. The series rerun with
this method is still **1 of 4**, and the diagnostic shows that number never measured page
retrieval: the years line up on 21 of 24, but the file the walk opens at that year is the one
the key cites on only **3** of the 21 — it has been measuring which copy the shelf calls the
canonical one.

**7. Edition selection, and whether the split is adopted.**
Structural set recall **3 of 17** against a bar of 15, mean set size 7.1. **The split is not
adopted.** The year branch fails all ten of its questions, and not because the rule is wrong:
of the eighteen evidence documents behind them, seven carry the asked year anywhere in their
metadata and one carries it as its primary year. A question about a fiscal year is answered by
whichever edition prints that year's row, usually a later one. The year narrows the row, not
the edition.

**8. Holdout confirmation.**
Everything that moved was checked on the 30 frozen holdout questions, aggregate counts only,
never per question. Pool recall at depth 100: **28 of 30**, confirming A0. Paraphrases in the
top ten: **14 of 30**, confirming that the paraphrase gain is real. The reranker: 12 of 30
alone, 16 of 30 fused — a genuine +2 over the baseline, which is why it is worth recording
that the stop was decided on the development set, as pre-registered, and not overridden by the
holdout. The caption and edition results were measured on the development set only, by design.

**9. The chain test.**
**Not run.** It needed the best document configuration to put the right publication in the top
three on at least 8 of 17; the best available does it on **5**. Running it anyway would have
measured the document bottleneck a fourth time through a more expensive harness.

**10. Each box.**
*Keep:* the shelf; absence detection in both directions — and it is the one box that survived
a deliberate attempt to break its measurement, since all eleven absence hits are genuine
missing-edition verdicts rather than failures to find the publication at all; cell
verification. *Weak:* the presence half of routing, at 8 of 11; the caption channel, which is a
real win on one question type and inert on the rest. *Failed:* finding the right document, at
9 of 17 against 12, and now failed a second way because the one untested idea for it did not
work; structural edition selection at 3 of 17; and the trajectory metric itself, which was
measuring the wrong thing. *Still untested:* the pipeline as one system — nothing here ran end
to end; the extraction router; the retry edge after a failed verification; the rule that the
answering model must open a page before citing it; and per-query maximum fusion, which is now
the best-supported untried idea in the repository.

**11. The biggest remaining bottleneck, in one sentence.**
Finding the right document is still the bottleneck, but it is now specifically a *ranking*
bottleneck with a known floor and a known ceiling — the right answer is in the top hundred on
sixteen of seventeen questions and in the top ten on nine, and neither a reranker nor an
edition rule closed that gap.

**12. The next experiment, if one is justified.**
One is. On **12 of 17** questions some single paraphrase, on its own, already puts the right
document in its own top ten, while all six fused together manage 9 — fusion is losing
questions its own inputs had, and the reranker experiment confirmed the same thing from the
other side. **Experiment C: score each publication by its best rank across the paraphrases
instead of by summed reciprocal rank, breaking ties on how many paraphrases found it.** No
model calls, no new index, about an hour. Pre-registered gate: pass at ≥12 of 17 *and* ≥17 of
30 on the holdout; weak at 10–11 and ≥16; stop at ≤9, or if the holdout does not improve by at
least 2. Do not tune the fusion constant, add a seventh paraphrase, or change the pool depth
to get past it.

generated corpus itself contains 52 decoy `CLAUDE.md` files dated between 2011 and August 2026,
