## How to use it

Open a terminal in the `research` folder and start Claude Code there:

```
cd research
claude --model claude-opus-5
```

Then ask in plain English — "how has spending on X changed over the last ten years?" An answer usually takes one to three minutes, because it's opening real pages before replying. Every number comes with a `Sources` line naming the file and page it came from, and when the folder doesn't contain the answer it says so instead of guessing. The first few commands will ask your permission to run; approving them is expected.

**The answers are only as good as this folder.** A `Sources` line is a claim you can check in seconds — open the file at that page. Do check a few early on.

If you'd rather not leave this terminal, the same question works here:

```
engine\.venv\Scripts\python.exe engine\bin\setup_folder.py ask --folder research "<your question>"
```

## First, what this answer is and isn't

The folder does **not** contain a single table of *total* provincial expenditure by province. What the Pakistan Economic Survey's statistical appendix does carry, edition by edition, is the **Annual Development Programme (ADP) by province** — that's development spending only — plus scattered current-expenditure lines for education and health. So the decade trend below is a **development-spending** trend, not all provincial spending. I've flagged where the current side differs.

All figures are **nominal rupees**. That matters enormously here, and I quantify it below.

## The headline: development spending roughly tripled since 2018-19

Annual Development Programme, Rs billion (2025-26 Survey vintage; Balochistan converted from the Rs million its table uses):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2018-19 | 290.3 | 113.0 | 70.0 | 34.2 | 507.5 |
| 2019-20 | 326.6 | 126.0 | 79.0 | 35.4 | 567.0 |
| 2020-21 | 365.3 | 148.0 | 91.0 | 44.2 | 648.5 |
| 2021-22 | 488.2 | 180.0 | 111.0 | 53.6 | 832.8 |
| 2022-23 | 582.9 | 217.0 | 139.0 | 67.1 | 1,005.9 |
| 2023-24 | 764.2 | 298.0 | 191.0 | 88.7 | 1,341.9 |
| 2024-25 (R) | 859.2 | 334.0 | 204.0 | 96.5 | 1,493.7 |
| 2025-26 (P) | 921.1 | 383.0 | 240.0 | 112.2 | 1,656.3 |

Over 2018-19 → 2025-26 the four-province total rose **3.26×**. Individually the multiples are strikingly similar: Punjab 3.17×, Sindh 3.39×, KP 3.43×, Balochistan 3.28×.

Going further back on the earlier vintage, Punjab's ADP was 198.3 in 2014-15 and KP's 50.0 — so across the full decade both grew roughly **4.6–4.8×** in nominal terms.

## Trend 1: almost all of it is inflation

This is the single most important qualifier. The rupee went from 137.46/US$ in 2018-19 to 286.08/US$ in 2025-26 — a 2.08× depreciation over exactly the period where nominal ADP rose 3.26×.

Converting the four-province total at those rates: **US$3.7bn in 2018-19 → US$5.8bn in 2025-26**, about **1.57×** rather than 3.26×. Headline CPI inflation ran 9.10% and 12.26% in 2020-21 and 2021-22 alone. A dollar conversion is not a proper deflator, but it makes the point: roughly half to two-thirds of the apparent tripling is price change, not more schools and roads.

## Trend 2: the shares across provinces barely moved

| Province | Share 2018-19 | Share 2025-26 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Punjab | 57.2% | 55.6% | −1.6 pp |
| Sindh | 22.3% | 23.1% | +0.9 pp |
| KP | 13.8% | 14.5% | +0.7 pp |
| Balochistan | 6.7% | 6.8% | +0.1 pp |

This is the clearest finding in the data: **the interprovincial distribution of development spending is essentially frozen.** Seven years of large nominal growth, and no province's share shifts by more than about 1.6 percentage points. Punjab drifts slightly down, KP and Sindh slightly up, Balochistan flat at roughly a fifteenth of the total.

## Trend 3: growth came in two bursts, and has now cooled

Year-on-year growth in the four-province total: +11.7% (2019-20), +14.4% (2020-21), **+28.4% (2021-22)**, +20.8% (2022-23), **+33.4% (2023-24)**, then +11.3% and +10.9% in the last two years.

Two surges — 2021-22 and a larger one in 2023-24 — with the most recent two years back down to ~11%, which given the inflation above means development spending is roughly flat or falling in real terms right now.

## Trend 4: development spending outpaced social current spending

Where both are available for Punjab over 2014-15 → 2021-22: ADP grew 2.47× while Health (Current) grew 2.25× (75.7 → 170.4 Rs billion). Balochistan Health (Current) grew 2.33× over the same window. On the longer view, KP Health (Current) grew 2.81× (2016-17 → 2023-24) and Sindh Education (Current) 2.90× — both below the ~3.2–3.4× of ADP. The tilt is modest but consistent: **the development budget grew somewhat faster than recurrent social spending.**

## Two data problems you should know about

**1. The Sindh series cannot be spliced.** The 2021-22 Survey prints Sindh ADP 2018-19 as **132.5** (Base 2005-06); the 2025-26 Survey prints the same year as **113.0** (Base 2015-16). That's a 17% gap, and it is a base-year change, not a revision. Any Sindh trend crossing those two editions is unreliable — I used the 2025-26 vintage throughout the main table for that reason.

**2. Ordinary revisions, one of them large.** KP's 2021-22 ADP was published as 100.0 (provisional) in the 2021-22 edition and 111.0 in the 2025-26 edition — revised up 11%. Punjab's 2020-21 (364.4 → 365.3) and 2021-22 (489.4 → 488.2), and Balochistan's 2018-19 (35.0 → 34.2), are trivial by comparison.
